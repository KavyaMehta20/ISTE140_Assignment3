Why sorting matters

Sorting is one of the first big ideas in computer science. Whenever an app shows your messages from newest to oldest, or a shop lists prices from low to high, a sorting algorithm did the work behind the scenes. Some methods are easy to understand but slow on large lists. Others are harder to write but finish in a fraction of the time. Here are five that every CS student should know, ranked from the simplest idea to the most clever one. Each card shows a small bar chart: the mint and violet bars are the ones being compared or moved.

My top five

#1

Bubble Sort

Swaps neighbours that are in the wrong order, so big values bubble to the end. Simple but slow.

#2

Selection Sort

Finds the smallest item left and puts it at the front, one position at a time.

#3

Insertion Sort

Builds a sorted section by sliding each new item into the right spot, like sorting playing cards.

#4

Merge Sort

Splits the list in half again and again, then merges the sorted halves back together.

#5

Quick Sort

Picks a pivot, puts smaller items on one side and bigger on the other, then repeats.

Study tips

Do not just memorise code. Draw a short list of six numbers on paper and run each algorithm by hand, step by step. Once you can predict every swap, writing the code becomes much easier.

Speed at a glance

Bubble Sort
O(n²)
Selection Sort
O(n²)
Insertion Sort
O(n²)
Merge Sort
O(n log n)
Quick Sort
O(n log n) average

Smaller growth means faster on big lists.
